How we work

Small on purpose.

Being small means we can look at every case and every project individually, and make sure each pound reaches the child or family it was given for.

IWAF is non-political, non-religious and non-racial. We help whoever asks, regardless of colour, religion or ethnicity, and we follow all the rules of the Charity Commission for England and Wales.
1

We go ourselves

Trustees and volunteers spend weeks at a time in Baghdad, at the schools, in family homes and at the centres we support. Even during Covid, our volunteers kept visiting.

2

We listen to the people who know

We start every visit with the headteachers and staff, so donations go to what children actually need: uniforms, coats, medicine, glasses, a meal.

3

We help families stand on their own

Alongside urgent help, we fund small businesses for widows, so families can earn an income and rely less on handouts.

4

We report back

After every trip we share what was done, with photos, with everyone who gave. Read the latest report.

Our work

Where your support goes

Most of our work happens in and around Baghdad, centred on the Al-Farah group of orphan schools for boys and girls, previously known as Kafil Al-Yateem.

Orphan sponsorship

£1 a day keeps one of the poorest children in school, and off the streets. A monthly lifeline for their family that costs less than a meal out in London.

£1 a day · £30 a month

Al-Farah orphan schools

Across the Al-Farah group of orphan schools in Baghdad (previously Kafil Al-Yateem) we fund school meals, clothing and medical support, so no child learns hungry, cold or unwell.

£10 a month feeds a child

Art expression workshops

Art-based sessions that build confidence, wellbeing and life goals for children affected by war and loss, led by artist and teacher Khulood Da'mi.

For four years and counting

Women's livelihoods

Bread-making machines, tanoor ovens, sewing machines and lessons, ice-cream carts and IT courses, so widows can build an income for their families.

Ramadan & emergency appeals

Iftar nights for orphans, food baskets for families in extreme poverty, and help building homes for widows and orphaned families.

Health & disability

Medicine and doctor's visits for children who can't afford them, eye glasses, and regular visits to centres for deaf and autistic children, the elderly and a children's cancer hospital.

Field report

Baghdad, November to December 2025

Months in the making. We shipped six boxes and two suitcases of coats, clothing, art supplies, games and stationery, then spent our weekdays at the orphan schools from 9am to 3pm and our weekends visiting widows, orphans and the families many of you sponsor.

For many, this was their first ever outing and their first ever earnings. Truly a life-changing day.
Amira, IWAF, on the girls' trip

Everything else we did

  • Paid for doctor's visits, medicine and eye glasses for students whose families couldn't afford them, guided by their headteachers.
  • Delivered gifts and school bags to Al-Farah 5 Primary and Secondary School for Orphans.
  • Gave out toys across the wards of a children's cancer hospital that treats under-18s from across Iraq.
  • Returned to the Centre for Deaf Children in Diwaniya with canvases, paints, cross-stitch kits and fruit.
  • Revisited women we had helped set up small businesses, now earning for their families.
  • Learned from Al-Taawn School, which gives 20% of its places to orphans for free.
Art expression

Art that helps children heal.

Khulood Da'mi is a renowned Iraqi artist, an art teacher by profession and a former trustee of IWAF. Since the 1990s she has trained and worked in therapy through art with children affected by war and violence.

She returns to Iraq to spend weeks at a time running at least four workshops a day for children aged 6 to 15 in orphan schools, refugee centres and centres for children with special needs. They make Ramadan lanterns, kites and butterfly wings, and they write in cuneiform, the script their ancestors invented.

"I can't wait to hug them all again."
Khulood, before her next trip
"In their first visit to the Iraq Museum, the girls stood in front of an artefact their ancestors created a thousand years before Pythagoras' theorem."Khulood Da'mi, at the Iraq Museum, Baghdad

Our story

Three decades, one promise.

1995

IWAF is founded in the UK to support impoverished Iraqi orphans, widows and displaced families with food, medicine, shelter, counselling and education.

2021

Through Covid, volunteers keep visiting families. Eid presents for the children, school uniforms, and a 16-home building project for widows and orphans north of Baghdad.

2022

An urgent clothing appeal for close to 700 children at Kafil Al-Yateem school (now Al-Farah), and art workshops with children at the autism centre in Karbala.

2024

Iftar for 280 orphans and a pioneering school trip to the Iraq Museum, where children met their own history for the first time.

2025

Iftar for 250 children in Ramadan, then five weeks in Baghdad in November and December: workshops, family visits, healthcare and trips to Babylon and Al-Wattar House.

2026

Ramadan food baskets for all 80 children of Al-Farah 5 orphan school and their families, each with rice, oil, flour, lentils, chickpeas, sugar, tea, milk, eggs and chicken.
